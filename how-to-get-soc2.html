How to get a SOC 2 report

A SOC 2 Type II report is an independent audit of a vendor's security controls. We ask for it because it is the clearest evidence that a tool handles data safely. There is no central website or repository of SOC 2 reports, and many vendors keep them behind a login. Since you already use (or plan to use) the tool, you are usually the fastest person to get it - so this page walks you through how.

Why we ask you and not ITS. There is no directory to look these up in, some vendors will only share the report with an account holder, and some do not have one at all. Rather than have ITS hunt down and request a report from a vendor we have no relationship with, the requestor grabs what they can reach. If you cannot get it, that is fine - tell us and we will take it from there.

1Check the vendor's Trust Center or Security page

This is where most vendors publish compliance documents. Find it by searching the web for:

  • "[vendor name] trust center" or "[vendor name] SOC 2" or "[vendor name] security"
  • Common addresses look like trust.vendor.com or vendor.com/security

On the trust page you will usually see the certifications the vendor holds (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001) and a button to request access to the actual report.

2Request or download the report

  • Most trust pages ask you to enter your Syracuse email and accept a short non-disclosure agreement (a checkbox or e-signature). After that they email you a link or let you download the PDF.
  • If you already have a login for the product, check its admin or security/compliance settings - some vendors post the report there for account holders.
  • If neither works, email the vendor's support or your account contact: "Please share your current SOC 2 Type II report."
If the report is behind a login. If the trust page or product requires an account to open the report, please download the PDF and send it to us with your request (or attach it). ITS often cannot log in to a vendor's product, but you likely can - so the file you can reach saves everyone a step.

3If you cannot get the full report, these public checks still help

These will not always give you the full SOC 2, but they are public and worth grabbing if you find them:

  • CSA STAR Registry (cloudsecurityalliance.org/star/registry) - a free, browsable list; search the vendor and note whether they are listed and at what level.
  • A SOC 3 report - a public summary some vendors post on their security page (no login needed).
  • ISO 27001 certificate - if the vendor claims it, the certificate can often be verified publicly.

4If the vendor does not have a SOC 2 report

That is common for smaller or open-source tools, and it is not an automatic no. Just tell us on the form. ITS will look at alternatives (for example an ISO 27001 certificate or a higher-education HECVAT), and for some cases a local connection in Claude Desktop - which keeps data on your own machine - may be the better route.

What to send us with your request:
  • The trust-page link, and
  • the report file (PDF) if the page needed a login to open it, or
  • a note that the vendor does not have one - whichever applies.
